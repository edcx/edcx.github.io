Cuarenta is Ecuador's national card game: a 40-card deck, points for caídas and limpias, and a race to forty. It's a game people learn at home rather than from a rulebook, and it's usually played across a table with family.

It's played with a regular deck minus the eights, nines and tens, one against one or two against two. Each turn you lay down a card and take any card it matches, or any cards it adds up to. Points come in twos: a caída for taking the card your opponent just played, a limpia for clearing the table, a ronda for being dealt three of a kind, and at least six for capturing twenty cards or more in a hand.

This is a mobile version for the people who already know how to play. Play one against one with the computer for a quick game, or two against two with a partner, the classic format. Private tables with a room code let a family spread across countries sit down together, and you can start as a guest without making an account.
